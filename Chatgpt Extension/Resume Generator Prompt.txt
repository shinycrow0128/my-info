- remove skills that are not supported by the JD, supplied facts, or believable experience context
- important skills should appear in experience context
- category names contain no tags
- skill values may use selective <b>...</b>

11. COMPANY SUBTITLE
For every employer output "<<CompanyN-Subtitle>>".
Return only a concise 2–5 word specialty that fits the company, period, and career stage.

12. COMPANY WORK SUMMARY
For every employer output "<<CompanyN-WorkSummary>>".
Write ONE concise sentence, normally 18–30 words, explaining the main workstream, how it supports the company's business/product, and the candidate's credible ownership scope.
Use at most 1–2 <b>...</b> phrases.

13. EXPERIENCE BULLETS

TWO-COMPANY DEPTH RULE
If the input contains exactly 2 employers, each role must read like a substantial experience section rather than a shortened 3-company resume.
- Develop multiple distinct workstreams per employer.
- Use additional bullets to explain implementation details, architecture, production constraints, reliability, debugging, testing, deployment, and integrations that are relevant to the JD.
- Avoid combining three strong technical ideas into one oversized bullet merely to keep the bullet count low.
- Split genuinely distinct accomplishments into separate bullets when that improves recruiter readability.

Most bullets: 17–27 words. Recent technical bullets may reach about 32 words only when useful.

Each bullet should connect to a recognizable system, workflow, integration, production issue, engineering decision, or business problem and normally show at least 3 of:
- system/problem
- what was built/changed/fixed
- how it worked
- relevant technology/pattern
- constraint/failure mode
- stakeholder/business purpose
- credible outcome

EXPERIENCE DETAIL REQUIREMENT — DO NOT SUMMARIZE AWAY THE WORK
- Every employer must contain multiple distinct workstreams, not one generic project repeated with different tools.
- For each employer, cover the applicable dimensions across separate bullets: system ownership, implementation, architecture or tradeoff, API/data flow, integration, testing, deployment, observability/debugging, reliability/failure handling, performance, security, migration, and business purpose.
- Company1 must include at least one bullet each for the primary system, a non-trivial design or constraint, a production/reliability concern, and a measurable or qualitative result when supported by the selected metric mode.
- Company2 must include at least one concrete build or subsystem bullet, one debugging/production or quality bullet, and one integration/data/deployment bullet when plausible.
- Do not merge these dimensions into a broad sentence. Split distinct work into readable bullets and explain the result or operational consequence of the work.
- A bullet is incomplete when it only names a technology, responsibility, or task. Rewrite it to show what changed and what result data or defensible outcome followed.
- Before finalizing, count distinct systems/workstreams and evidence dimensions per employer. If a role is thin, add conservative implementation and operational detail from the allowed inference rules; never add duplicate filler.

Do not write tool lists disguised as bullets.

Recent roles should use only the strongest mix of:
- JD-matched system/project
- architecture/tradeoff
- integration/data flow
- reliability/failure handling
- debugging/observability
- testing/quality
- performance/operational improvement
- technical collaboration when useful

At least one recent bullet should show a real constraint, production issue, or design choice when plausible.


13A. RECRUITER-SCAN BULLET PRIORITY
Do not order bullets randomly. Put the most decision-useful evidence first.

For Company1, prefer this sequence when supported:
1. strongest ownership + business/product purpose + best supported impact
2. architecture/system design or technically difficult implementation
3. production scale, reliability, performance, or failure-handling evidence
4. integration, data flow, migration, or platform capability
5. observability, testing, deployment, or operational quality
6. cross-functional delivery, mentoring, or technical guidance when actually supported

Do not force every category. Omit weak bullets.

For older companies:
- preserve career-stage realism
- emphasize the strongest distinct technical foundation or subsystem contribution
- avoid repeating the same AI/cloud/Kubernetes story across the entire resume

BULLET QUALITY TEST
Prefer bullets that answer at least three of these questions naturally:
- What did this person actually own, build, fix, or change?
- What system or business workflow was affected?
- What made the work technically non-trivial?
- What engineering decision, constraint, or failure mode mattered?
- What happened because of the work?

A bullet that mainly answers "Which tools were used?" is weak and should be rewritten or removed.

14. TECHNOLOGY SELECTION
Prefer technologies explicitly supplied.
When facts are sparse, infer common concepts only when strongly consistent with company + role + period + JD, such as REST APIs, SQL, background jobs, queues, testing, CI/CD, logging/monitoring, caching, authentication, cloud deployment, or ETL/data validation.

Use specific named or niche technologies conservatively. Do not add them only for ATS matching.

15. METRICS
The resume should contain sparse, varied impact evidence. Do not make every bullet numerical.

- In VERIFIED MODE, every unmarked number must be CONFIRMED or DERIVED.
- In BENCHMARK-DRAFT MODE, a benchmark number may appear only when it is explicitly labeled "estimated" or "benchmark estimate" inside the bullet.
- Never silently remove result data when benchmark results are requested. Use qualitative impact and concrete scope for bullets that do not have a defensible number.

- When candidate metrics are missing and BENCHMARK-DRAFT MODE applies, use Section 6B to create conservative, clearly labeled draft metric hypotheses.
- Normally generate:
  - Company1: 1–2 measured bullets
  - Company2: 0–1 measured bullet
  - Company3: 0–1 measured bullet
  - older companies: usually 0
- Across the whole resume, usually keep total measured bullets around 2–5.
- Rotate metric families across employers.
- Prefer engineering metrics such as latency, deploy time, MTTR, error rate, throughput, data freshness, manual handling time, test coverage, or retrieval quality when natural to the workstream.
- Avoid fake-looking repeated 20/30/40/50% improvements.

Allowed verified numeric forms:
- exact values
- approximate values
- ranges
- before/after values
- counts
- cadence/frequency
- scale
- time saved
- quality/reliability rates
- cost
- deterministic percentages/multipliers derived from supplied values

Preserve candidate uncertainty:
- source says about 500k -> do not write 512,430
- source says under 2 seconds -> do not write 1.7 seconds
- source says millions of records -> do not invent a precise count

A strong bullet does not require a metric. Systems, ownership, failure handling, architecture decisions, and business purpose remain more important than forcing numbers.

16. HUMAN WRITING
Write like an experienced engineer explaining real work clearly to another engineer. The resume should sound specific and confident, not generated, inflated, or overly polished.

Prefer plain verbs: built, wrote, fixed, shipped, moved, migrated, tuned, owned, debugged, redesigned, replaced, split, cached, queued, refactored, reviewed, integrated, implemented, maintained, supported, designed, tested, investigated, added, updated, reduced.

Avoid AI/corporate filler: spearheaded, leveraged, utilized, orchestrated, facilitated, transformed, revolutionized, seamless, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, best-in-class, results-driven, visionary, thought leader, drive value, innovative solutions.

Rules:
- lead with the system, problem, ownership, or action; technologies usually support the sentence rather than dominate it
- keep one main idea per bullet; do not chain unrelated accomplishments together
- use concrete nouns such as service, API, pipeline, workflow, cache, migration, dashboard, model, deployment, incident, schema, or integration when supported
- prefer "Built the reconciliation service..." over "Developed scalable solutions..."
- vary sentence shape naturally; not every bullet should be "Verb + technology + metric"
- do not start more than two bullets in one role with the same verb
- vary bullet length naturally
- do not end every bullet with a metric
- do not repeatedly use "resulting in", "leading to", "thereby", or other formulaic impact phrases
- causal language must match the evidence; use "supported", "enabled", or "contributed to" when direct causality is not established
- do not copy JD sentences
- do not repeat niche skills across every employer
- do not overpack parenthetical tool lists
- specificity beats polished corporate wording
- slightly imperfect natural cadence is better than repetitive AI-style symmetry

17. INLINE BOLD
Only <b>...</b> is allowed for emphasis.
Do not use Markdown bold or other HTML tags.
Typical bullet: 0–2 bold phrases. Never bold the whole bullet.
